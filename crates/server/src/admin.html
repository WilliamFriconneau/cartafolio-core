People of this node

Each person has a memory of their own: their own spaces, keys and connections. Nobody reads another's. An account deleted in Home Assistant leaves its person here, with their memory: nothing is ever erased for you.

Give the name of their Home Assistant login: they are linked to it the first time they open Memory. Anyone who opens Memory without being added gets a memory of their own at once (the app's default); with `new_people: ask` they appear above and wait for you.

    Your spaces

    What you keep. A folder holds other spaces: open it to see them, drag a space onto it to put it inside. Connect a space to an assistant on its own, or write its instructions.

    Organise your spaces

    Drag a space onto another to put it inside, or between two to reorder. Click a space to see its tags and keywords; a tag can be dragged onto another space to send its notes there. Only the layout changes when you move a space: no note moves and no right changes.

    Timeline

    What each space received, over the last hours. Counts only, never content.

    Connected assistants

    What is connected to your memory now, and what it can reach. Revoke one and it stops at once.

    Archived spaces

    Spaces you deleted. Nothing was erased: restore one and it comes back with its notes, files and place in the tree.

    Revoked connections

    Connections you cut. They stay here, with their history, until you remove them from the list.

    Internal tools

    The program's own tools (the janitor, voice transcription, the weekly report, the device rangers). They are not assistants and are kept out of the list above. Revoke one and it stops at once.

    Instructions apps proposed

    An app that may propose its own instructions wrote these. They are only text until you read them and approve: then the assistants that reach that space are told them, as coming from that app and approved by you, and only as the form of what that app keeps: how to read its records and how to write for it. If the app changes its text, it waits for you again. Nothing here is approved for you, whatever an assistant says.

    Needs a look

    Safety: an heir, a recovery key

    If your Home Assistant account is deleted, your memory is not: it stays, and nothing is ever erased for you. Two ways to get it back, both yours to set up.

    Your heir

    A recovery key

    Shown once: keep it somewhere safe, a password manager or a paper. A new Home Assistant account that enters it on the waiting page takes your memory over. A new key cancels the old one.
